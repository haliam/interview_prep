# 05 — Interview Communication Guide

> **Purpose:** How to say things, not what to answer — delivery, language, positioning, and the questions you ask.

---

## Part 1 — English Phrases for the Interview

### First impressions matter

Start your interview with confidence:

- "Good morning/afternoon. My name is [Your Name], thank you for this opportunity."
- "Hello, I'm [Your Name]. It's a pleasure to be here."
- "Good morning/afternoon. I appreciate your consideration for this position."

💡 **Tip:** Mention something positive about the company or role to show genuine interest.

### Asking to repeat a question

If you didn't understand, stay calm and polite:

- "I'm sorry, could you please repeat the question?"
- "Excuse me, I didn't hear you. Could you say that again?"
- "I didn't catch the last part. Could you repeat it, please?"

💡 **Tip:** These phrases show confidence and clear communication skills.

### Emphasizing your strengths

Highlight your value with tailored phrases:

- "I believe I'm the right choice because I know how to [Skill/Task]."
- "My [Skill] will help me succeed in this role."
- "I'm very good at [Skill], which makes me confident about this position."
- "I'd love to work here because I [Motivation/Interest]."
- "My top three skills are [Skill 1], [Skill 2], and [Skill 3]."

💡 **Tip:** Always back up strengths with real-life examples.

### Ending the interview politely

Leave a strong final impression:

- "Thank you very much for your time. I look forward to your response."
- "I truly appreciate this opportunity."
- "It was a pleasure meeting you. Thank you for your time."
- "Thanks again, I'll be expecting your call."

### Additional useful phrases

- "I've been looking for a position like this for a long time."
- "This role aligns with my goals and skills."
- "I'm excited about working with your team."
- "You can expect professionalism and great results if you hire me."

### Describing yourself

**Personality traits:** Easy-going, hard-working, committed, trustworthy, honest, focused, methodical, proactive.

- Example: "I'm a very focused and methodical worker."

**Strengths valued by managers:** Organization, multitasking, meeting deadlines, problem solving, communication, working internationally, foreign languages, enthusiasm.

- Example: "I'm skilled at working under pressure and meeting tight deadlines."

### Talking about experience

- "I have five years' experience as a [Role]."
- "I worked in [Industry] for [X years] and was promoted to [Position]."
- "I studied at [University]."
- "I worked for [Company] as a [Position]."

💡 **Tip:** Be ready to expand on your CV with specific examples.

### Expressing future goals and motivation

- "I'm looking to develop my skills in [Industry/Role]."
- "I want to grow my career as a [Profession]."
- "I admire your company's work in [Industry]."
- "I believe I'm a great fit and can contribute by [Specific Value]."

### Final tip

> "I am very demanding at work and enthusiastic about continuous learning."
> This shows strong work ethic and willingness to grow.

✨ Stay calm, be clear, and let your personality and skills shine!

## Part 2 — Smart Questions to Ask the Interviewer

1. "You have probably been interviewing a lot of people that could do the job — but from your point of view, what does a high performer look like in this role?"
2. "What specific skills would justify offering the top end of the salary range for this role?"
3. "What is the most urgent business problem this role is expected to solve in the first 3–6 months?"
4. "What would an exceptional first two months look like for someone in this position?"
5. "Before we finish, is there anything in my background you'd like me to expand on to help you see me as a strong fit for the role?"
6. "How will success be measured for this role during the first 60 days and the first year?"
7. "What challenges has the team struggled with recently that this role is expected to improve?"
8. "How does the company support high performers in terms of career progression, compensation reviews, and visibility?"
9. "What qualities do you believe are essential for someone to thrive in the culture of this team?"
10. "What would you say are the biggest opportunities for innovation or improvement in this department right now?"

## Part 3 — Top-3 Candidate Positioning Strategy

How to sell yourself as a top 1% candidate.

### 1. Position yourself as the "Engineering Multiplier"

You don't just code — you **increase team performance**.
Use lines like:
"I focus on improving engineering velocity, not just delivering features."

This is rare and extremely valuable in UK tech teams.

### 2. Highlight your measurable impact

Your CV is full of numbers. Use them aggressively:

- 60% productivity boost
- 98% delivery rate
- 90% code coverage
- 40% performance improvements
- 30% onboarding reduction

UK hiring managers LOVE metrics.

### 3. Sell your hybrid value: Senior engineer + Tech Lead

You're not "just" a developer. You're a developer who can:

- lead
- mentor
- improve processes
- align with stakeholders
- deliver consistently

This places you in the **top salary bracket** (see [04_salary_negotiation.md](04_salary_negotiation.md)).
