# 03 — STAR Behavioural Interview Stories

> **Purpose:** Nine reusable stories covering the most common behavioural questions. Keep all metrics verbatim.
> **Structure:** Situation → Task → Action → Result.

---

## 1. "Tell me about a time you improved team productivity."

**Situation:**
My team at INETUM was struggling with slow delivery cycles and inconsistent code quality.

**Task:**
As the Senior Engineer and Tech Lead, I needed to improve productivity and reduce blockers.

**Action:**
I introduced GitHub Copilot, standardised clean architecture practices, and improved our CI/CD pipeline. I also led sprint refinement sessions to clarify requirements earlier.

**Result:**
Productivity increased by **60%**, blockers dropped by **25%**, and the team reached a **98% delivery rate**.

---

## 2. "Describe a time you improved code quality."

**Situation:**
When I joined the project, code coverage was only 35% and defects were frequent.

**Task:**
My goal was to raise code quality and make the system more maintainable.

**Action:**
I introduced automated testing, refactored key modules using clean architecture, and coached the team on SOLID principles.

**Result:**
Code coverage increased from **35% to 90%**, and defect rates dropped significantly, improving stability and developer confidence.

---

## 3. "Tell me about a time you handled a challenging technical problem."

**Situation:**
We needed to build an AI avatar for MWC using Azure OpenAI, with strict performance requirements.

**Task:**
I was responsible for designing and implementing a scalable solution.

**Action:**
I designed an Azure-based architecture using Functions, App Services, and caching strategies to optimise response times.

**Result:**
The solution reduced user wait times by **30%** and performed reliably during high-traffic demos.

---

## 4. "Tell me about a time you worked under pressure or tight deadlines."

**Situation:**
A client requested major feature changes late in the sprint.

**Task:**
I needed to deliver quickly without compromising quality.

**Action:**
I broke the work into smaller deliverables, aligned with stakeholders to prioritise the highest-impact items, and coordinated the team to avoid duplication.

**Result:**
We delivered the critical features on time, with no production issues, and the client was extremely satisfied.

---

## 5. "Describe a time you helped someone grow or mentored a colleague."

**Situation:**
Several mid-level developers struggled with architectural decisions and testing practices.

**Task:**
As Tech Lead, I needed to support their growth and improve team autonomy.

**Action:**
I created short architecture guidelines, ran pair-programming sessions, and reviewed their code with a focus on teaching rather than correcting.

**Result:**
Their confidence and output improved noticeably, onboarding time dropped by **30%**, and the team became more self-sufficient.

---

## 6. "Tell me about a time you improved a legacy system."

**Situation:**
At Compensa, we had legacy .NET modules that slowed delivery and caused frequent regressions.

**Task:**
My goal was to modernise the codebase and improve performance.

**Action:**
I refactored key modules using CQRS, introduced clean code practices, and added missing documentation.

**Result:**
Query performance improved by **40%**, delivery time dropped by **20%**, and maintainability increased significantly.

---

## 7. "Tell me about a time you showed leadership."

**Situation:**
Our time-record application grew very quickly, with many developers contributing to it. The application is used by more than **12,000 people** across multiple companies, but controllers had no consistent naming or implementation pattern.

**Task:**
I set myself the goal of standardizing controller naming and implementation to make the codebase easier to maintain and onboard into.

**Action:**
I created a common naming and implementation pattern for controllers and proposed it to our architect, who liked the idea. I then delivered the standardization.

**Result:**
The learning curve for any new developer is now shorter, and we facilitated the maintenance and growth of the application.

---

## 8. "Tell me about a time you took initiative / were successful on a team."

**Situation:**
When I started working at the company, it was difficult to understand all the stages and steps of our development cycle. I realized that even colleagues who had been there longer still had gaps in understanding it.

**Task:**
Nobody had asked for it, but it seemed necessary to make the process visible and clear for everyone.

**Action:**
I made a couple of diagrams where the whole development process was explained. I also built a custom dropdown template and carried out a big refactoring.

**Result:**
The diagrams were a success — several colleagues congratulated me on the delivery — and they have been genuinely helpful to the team ever since.

---

## 9. "Tell me about a time you had to manage conflicting priorities."

**Situation:**
Occasionally a new task arrives at the end of the sprint — normally a bug with some impact on the client — while sprint work is still in progress.

**Task:**
I needed to deliver both on time without silently absorbing an unsustainable workload.

**Action:**
I always ask to review the priority of the tasks. When the same priority has been maintained in two tasks, I have redoubled efforts and managed to deliver on time — but I inform the team and the PO that this is not a good practice and should not be repeated.

**Result:**
Deliveries were made on time, and the team learned that two equally critical tasks should not be assigned to the same developer. In our profession it is difficult to attack two tasks at the same time with the same intensity — there is always a hierarchy.
