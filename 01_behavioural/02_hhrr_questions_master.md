## What Recruiters Evaluate

Recruiters care about four things, always in this order:

1. **Can you do the job?** → technical fit, experience, skills
2. **Will you accept the offer?** → salary expectations, notice period, right to work
3. **Are you reliable and easy to place?** → communication style, stability
4. **Will the client like you?** → culture fit, motivation, soft skills

## Part 2 — Motivation and Career Goals

### Why do you want to work here?

**Framework (three steps):**

1. **Show** you actually read the job description: talk about the offer's key points and why they appeal to you.
2. **Show** genuine interest: find the points that really motivate you to pick that job and why.
3. **Connect** to your career story: show how this role lets you put into practice what you are good at.

> "I'm genuinely excited about this opportunity because it looks like a really good fit for what I enjoy doing and what I am good at."

### Why are you leaving your current role?

> "I am looking for new challenges in a more cloud-focused, product-driven environment where I can have a bigger impact on architecture, scalability, and engineering culture. I am always motivated by knowledge, I want to integrate the use of English in my daily work and learn about new technologies. I would love to work in a company with people from different countries who want to do things well."

### What is your ideal job?

> My ideal job would be in a dynamic and collaborative environment where continuous learning and innovation are encouraged. I thrive in teams that embrace Agile methodologies and focus on high-quality software using modern technologies. I'm passionate about contributing strategically and solving complex problems. A forward-thinking company that values growth and collaboration would be perfect for me.

### Where do you see yourself in 5 years?

**Framework:** Company career → Growing → Ambitions → Results

> "In the first one or two years, I plan to immerse myself in the position and add value through best practices, clean code, good documentation, and teamwork. In five years, I see myself in a leadership role, possibly as a tech lead or even in an architect position — a position where I could have responsibility for big tech decisions and guarantee the quality of development. This company and project look like they have the space to grow and achieve all these goals. The important thing is to give your best every day, stay focused, and enjoy the process."

### Why should we hire you?

**Framework:** Expertise → Leadership → Results → Impact

1. Identify what they are looking for in tech skills and what they need to solve or improve.
2. Show how you used those skills to bring results.
3. Link it to how you could help them.

> "You should hire me because I bring a strong combination of technical expertise, leadership experience, and clear communication, all consistently driven by results and quality. I have a solution-oriented mindset and a strong focus on continuous improvement.
>
> In my previous role, I led a team that delivered a critical project ahead of schedule while also identifying and implementing process improvements that increased team productivity significantly. This experience has given me the ability to quickly understand complex environments, remove obstacles, and create measurable impact.
>
> I'm confident that I can apply this same approach to your project by identifying blockers early, addressing limitations proactively, and delivering tangible improvements within the first two to three months."

💡 **Personal example of initiative:**
> "I created diagrams to explain our development process without being asked — they helped the whole team. I often find solutions where others see problems. I pursue my goals persistently and I enjoy challenges."

### What motivates you as an engineer?

## Part 5 — Behavioural and Pressure Questions

### How do you handle stress or pressure?

**Framework:** Order → Priorities → Results → Impact

**What it shows:** You are capable of prioritising the important tasks — identify what should be done first, ask for help if needed, and increase the pace if needed to achieve deadlines.

> "I handle stress and pressure by first staying organized and breaking down tasks into manageable steps. I also prioritize effectively and maintain open communication with my team to ensure everyone is aligned and informed. Additionally, I make it a point to take short breaks to recharge and maintain a healthy work-life balance."

💡 *Example: PUA preproduction delivery.*

### Tell me about a time you had to manage conflicting priorities

> "Occasionally a new task arrives at the end of the sprint — normally a bug with some impact on the client. If this happens, I always ask to review the priority of the tasks. When the same priority has been maintained in two tasks, I have redoubled efforts and managed to deliver on time, but I inform the team and the PO that this is not a good practice and should not be repeated. In our profession it is difficult to attack two tasks at the same time with the same intensity — there is always a hierarchy. If exceptionally there are two tasks that have the same priority, they should not be assigned to the same developer."

### How do you handle tight deadlines?

> "I prioritise clarity, break work into manageable pieces, and ensure the team focuses on high-impact tasks. I also use automation and testing to avoid regressions."

### How do you handle disagreements in the team?

> "I focus on data, best practices, and the long-term maintainability of the system. I encourage open discussion and aim for solutions that benefit the team and the product."

### How do you collaborate with stakeholders?

> "I keep communication clear, set expectations early, and translate technical details into business impact. At INETUM, this helped align the team and reduce blockers by 25%."
